# Engineering: Ohm's Law

**Question:** How does Ohm's law apply in a simple circuit?

---

**Goal:** Understand the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in an electrical circuit.

**Plain Language:**
Think of electricity like water flowing through a hose. 
- **Voltage (V)** is the water pressure from the pump pushing the water.
- **Current (I)** is the actual flow of water through the hose.
- **Resistance (R)** is how narrow or kinked the hose is, resisting the flow.
Ohm's Law simply states that the flow of water (current) increases if you increase the pump pressure (voltage) and decreases if you narrow the hose (resistance).

**Steps:**
1. **The Formula:**
   Ohm's law is mathematically defined as:
   $$ V = I \times R $$
   Where:
   - $V$ is Voltage, measured in Volts (V).
   - $I$ is Current, measured in Amperes or Amps (A).
   - $R$ is Resistance, measured in Ohms ($\Omega$).
2. **Applying it to a Circuit:**
   Imagine a simple circuit with a 9-Volt battery connected to a lightbulb with a resistance of 3 Ohms. 
3. **Calculating Current:**
   We want to find out how much current is flowing through the bulb. We rearrange the formula to solve for $I$:
   $$ I = \frac{V}{R} $$
   Substitute the values:
   $$ I = \frac{9\text{V}}{3\Omega} = 3\text{A} $$
   So, 3 Amperes of current flow through the circuit.

**Check:**
If the resistance stays the same (3 $\Omega$) but we double the voltage to an 18-Volt battery, what happens?
$I = \frac{18}{3} = 6\text{A}$. The current doubles. This matches our intuition: more "pressure" leads to more "flow," verifying the proportional relationship established by Ohm's Law.

**Takeaway:**
Ohm's Law ($V = IR$) is the fundamental rule for simple circuits, defining how the push of the battery (Voltage) and the pushback of the components (Resistance) determine exactly how much electricity flows (Current).
